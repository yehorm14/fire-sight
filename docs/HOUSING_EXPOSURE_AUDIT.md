# Housing exposure and feature audit

Reviewed 26 November 2026 using Fire Sight's versioned SFRS ward panel. Keep the existing forecasts. December stock supports retrospective rates for 2024/25 and 2025/26. The matching 2022 DataZone stock was published too late for any of the held-out forecasts. A separately labelled legacy sensitivity on the only eligible target found a small MAE gain from exposure, a higher RMSE and fewer observed fires captured in the five highest-ranked wards. For **observed** financial year `y/y+1`, divide the number of attended dwelling-fire incidents by December stock in year `y`. December is a within-year stock proxy. The Assessors' valuation list covers total dwellings, including vacant dwellings and eligible second homes. SFRS counts incidents, including repeat visits to the same dwelling in different incidents, so this rate measures incident frequency and cannot be read as the chance that a dwelling has a fire. NRS September occupied estimates come from Council Tax billing, use a different reference date and exclude vacant dwellings. They are available for context. We use total December stock because the outcome includes all dwelling fires. [NRS methodology, pp. 3, 7 and 13](https://nrscotland.gov.uk/media/qxrd2qq3/household-estimates-2025-methodology-guide.pdf)

## Held-out comparison

The candidate evaluation rolls from 2018/19 and reserves 2023/24–2025/26 as the fixed holdout from the 2022/23 origin. This audit uses the same 31 March origin and lead definitions. **The identical-geography 2022-DZ stock is unavailable at every scored origin**, including the final origin on 31 March 2025. The December 2024 stock was first published on 26 June 2025, and December 2025 stock on 30 July 2026. Neither enters a fitted model. [Candidate forecast evaluation](FORECAST_EVALUATION.md)

The 20 June 2024 2011-DZ release makes **one rolling target** eligible for a legacy sensitivity. The model forecasts FY2025/26 from 31 March 2025 for 23 wards. Its legacy denominators are historic 2011 DataZone stock grouped to WD24 with the January 2025 lookup. The numerator uses the revised SFRS counts with 2022 DataZone allocation. The experiment checks release availability for the housing inputs and uses the same revised fire labels as the candidate evaluation. The paired comparison does not establish operational real-time accuracy. The fixed 2022/23 origin and leads two and three have no eligible audited stock release.

Use observed December stock for historic training years available by the origin. Carry the last released stock, December 2023, into both FY2024/25 training and every forecast-period offset. Apply the same last-available rule to September 2023 vacancy. For future forecasts, keep this rule until a separate stock forecast can be fit and evaluated with the same release-date controls. Exclude the incomplete December 2022 year from both matched arms. Keep the full-history counts-only result as an anchor.

The matched models use a shared time slope and one intercept per ward, with an optional log stock offset. The vacancy trial adds one time-varying percentage, vacant dwellings divided by **September Council Tax stock from the same source**. All comparisons round each ward prediction to a whole count before aggregating. The top five use ward code as the tie-break.

| Model | Ward MAE | Ward RMSE | Ward bias | City prediction and error | Observed fires in predicted top five |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Full-history counts only | 4.00 | 5.10 | +1.39 | 648 (+32) | 185 |

Relative to the matched counts-only comparison, exposure swaps one predicted top-five ward and captures seven fewer observed fires. Adding vacancy on the matched shorter window keeps the same five wards. Both tests amount to one city year, with positive city bias across all variants. Use the rates for descriptive planning and keep the count forecast unchanged until more comparable target years can be scored.
